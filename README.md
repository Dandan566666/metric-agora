# Metric Agora

**The numbers shape our lives. The evidence should be open to everyone.**

## Why this project exists

A new report appears. A number travels across our screens. Before the day is over, it is shaping headlines, arguments, expectations, and decisions about the future.

Behind that number are people looking for work, families deciding whether they can afford a home, students choosing a path, patients waiting for care, and communities trying to understand what is happening around them.

**When a number is released, everyone should have a way to examine its truthfulness before accepting its story.**

When a leader, public official, executive, or anyone speaking for an institution cites a number, the people hearing it should have a practical way to check its source and supporting evidence. The position and reputation of the speaker cannot replace an examination of the claim itself.

Imagine opening a report, pasting a claim, or pointing to a chart and asking for its evidence. Where did the figure originate? What does it actually measure? Which people or activities does it leave out? How does it fit with independent observations? What would change our understanding of it?

Imagine being able to follow those questions across languages and borders: from births to school enrolment, from employment to hours and household income, from production to energy and freight, from public spending to the services people receive. Imagine finding the missing denominator, the repeated upstream source, the revision that changed the picture, or the omitted series that makes a confident headline much less certain.

**That ability should be available to the people whose lives these numbers describe.**

Population. Births. Jobs. Prices. Housing. Health. Food. Energy. Debt. Education. The environment. Public spending. These numbers help us understand the world and make decisions that reach into everyday life.

For most people, receiving data has meant accepting a fragment: a press release, a census result, a sample survey, a chart, a headline. We can read the result without being able to inspect how it fits with everything else. Which discrepancy is a harmless difference in definition? Which is a coverage gap, a calculation error, a distorted presentation, or a claim the available evidence cannot support?

The world is connected even when its data are published separately. Births, deaths, and migration relate to population change. Employment, hours, wages, and household spending illuminate different parts of economic life. Energy use, freight, production, and emissions offer other perspectives. These relationships create opportunities for cross-checking, provided their assumptions and limits are made explicit.

People have always investigated these connections. Doing so across institutions, languages, formats, periods, and disciplines demands enormous time and expertise. Much of the evidence remains scattered beyond what a small team can reasonably examine.

AI offers an opportunity to extend that work: finding candidate sources, reading documentation, extracting observations, proposing comparisons, and helping investigators follow inconsistencies across a much wider range of evidence. Careful statistical methods and independent review must establish which comparisons and conclusions hold up.

**Metric Agora is an invitation to build that capability as a public good.**

We want to connect evidence published by statistical offices, financial institutions, census bodies, survey organizations, research institutes, and every other public, private, academic, international, or community publisher whose data can lawfully contribute. Official statistics, administrative records, independent studies, censuses, and sample surveys should become discoverable within a shared map. We want to discover where measurements agree, where they diverge, what explains the difference, and what remains unknown. Where selective presentation or distortion can be demonstrated, we want readers to see exactly how it occurred.

Our ambition is to approach reality through sustained cross-checking, while making every step open to challenge. Sometimes the most honest result will be a range, several competing explanations, or an explicit statement that the truth is not identifiable from the available data.

We are inviting people everywhere to make scrutiny a normal part of public life. Each newly released figure should be an opportunity to examine evidence. Each discovered error should be an opportunity to improve what we know. Every person who brings a source, a method, a correction, or a question can help build that capacity.

## What people could build

Anyone who questions a figure from an official release or any other channel should be able to bring it forward for examination. A future investigation should ask both whether the published figure is supported and what values the independent evidence can actually justify, including a defensible range when a single value cannot be established. Questions can lead to confirmation, correction, a better-supported estimate, or an honest unresolved result.

A future tool could turn a question into an inspectable evidence dossier: original sources, comparable definitions, source dependencies, reproducible calculations, alternative explanations, and a conclusion with visible uncertainty. It could help people identify unsupported narratives and trace errors to particular observations or transformations.

The [research map](DATA_DOMAINS.md) describes 36 publisher families and 84 possible research directions, spanning population and family life, work, household welfare, production, finance, public institutions, health, education, food, land, energy, environment, infrastructure, technology, and human security. It is an invitation to investigate connections, not a claim that every relationship permits a valid estimate.

## Commitments worth carrying forward

- Every important factual claim should lead back to identifiable evidence and a stated method.
- Differences in definitions, coverage, timing, and revisions must be examined before alleging distortion. An anomaly alone does not establish intent or fraud.
- Ten websites repeating one upstream dataset provide one source family, not ten independent confirmations. Agreement among AI models also does not establish independent evidence.
- Observations, estimates, assumptions, interpretations, and unknowns must be distinguishable. A model's confident wording is not a calibrated probability.
- A dataset can be useful for one question and unsuitable for another. Assess specific claims and uses rather than assigning institutions a universal truth score.
- Community votes may determine priorities and rules. Evidence standards determine whether a conclusion is supported; dissent and counterevidence remain visible.
- Publicly accessible material still requires attention to its reuse conditions and privacy. Aggregate analysis must not expose individuals or vulnerable groups.

## Start with one question

An initial investigation could ask: **Does a falling unemployment rate demonstrate improving employment conditions?** Examine participation, employment relative to population, hours, wages, coverage, and revisions in the same place and period. The first useful contribution might be a methodological note or a manually checked comparison, before anyone writes software.

The [first design plan](DESIGN.md) offers milestones and acceptance criteria. Future communities can revise it through their own published decision process.

## Build a community of your own

Programmers can connect evidence. Statisticians can test methods. Researchers can explain context. Journalists can follow sources. Translators can bridge languages. A person who carefully checks one table can uncover a mistake that changes an entire conclusion.

Fork this seed, publish who is responsible for your fork, open a discussion space there, and invite people to help choose its direction. The [participation guide](CONTRIBUTING.md) explains how. The [governance proposal](GOVERNANCE.md) provides a starting point for participants to adapt; it is not an operating system already installed in this repository. No fork is automatically the official successor, and no endorsement by the initiator is implied.

**These numbers describe our lives. Helping them become more accurate is work worth sharing.**
