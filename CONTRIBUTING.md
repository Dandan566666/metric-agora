## Bring one question into the light

You may arrive with a dataset, a statistical method, a language, a skill, or a question you cannot stop asking. That can be enough to begin. Somewhere in a report is a definition another reader missed. Somewhere in a historical table is a revision that changes the story. Your care in finding it can give others firmer ground on which to stand.

This invitation reaches beyond job titles and borders. Join people who are willing to check, explain, listen, and change their minds when the evidence changes. Help create a place where a careful contribution can travel further than its author ever could alone.

## Where to participate

This original seed is intentionally unmaintained after publication. A pull request or issue here does not come with a review or response commitment. To develop the idea, create a fork and organize participation there. The original publisher does not need to approve your community, choose its members, or nominate a successor.

## Start an independent community

1. Fork the repository and keep its attribution and license notices.
2. Replace the fork's opening status notice with an accurate account of its maintainers, contact or discussion location, current capabilities, and relationship to this seed. Mark substantive changes.
3. Publish consenting custodians, access arrangements, conflicts of interest, and a transparent provisional decision process. If using an organization, establish ownership continuity.
4. Invite people to review the design and adopt or amend a charter. Make clear which rules are proposals and which are actually enforced.
5. Choose a small, tractable question from the research map. Publish a manually checked dossier before committing to software or broad claims.

Do not claim to be the sole official continuation or imply endorsement by the original initiator. This seed includes no directory of active forks; people evaluating a fork should inspect its current activity, rules, and evidence themselves.

## Useful first contributions

Document a source and its definitions. Identify an upstream dependency. Check a table against the original release. Explain a revision. Translate a methodological note. Reproduce a calculation. Supply a credible alternative explanation. Describe a failed comparison and what it taught you. Design an accessible way to show uncertainty. Help people agree on governance and review practices.

You do not have to write code to contribute. State what you checked, what you could not check, and any relevant affiliation or interest.

## A proposal should include

The question or problem; the change you propose; relevant sources and reuse conditions; definitions, period, and geography; comparison assumptions; counterevidence and limitations; acceptance criteria; and any resource or maintenance commitment requested. For a factual correction, identify the exact claim and evidence that changes it. Protect personal and confidential information when reporting a concern.

## Working together

Challenge claims with evidence and methods. Apply the same standard to sources you like and sources you distrust. Make room for corrections, disagreements, and people working across languages. Avoid harassment, doxxing, fabricated citations, and unsupported accusations. Moderation and appeals need named responsible people in each maintained community; the original initiator provides no ongoing moderation service.

## Reuse and future development

Original documents in this seed are under CC BY 4.0; retain attribution, link the license, and indicate changes. Source material remains subject to its own conditions. A future community should explicitly select licenses for new software and data contributions rather than assume this documentation license is suitable for every artifact. Do not upload credentials or set up costs in another person's name.

## Begin where you can

Choose one source. Check one number. Explain one discrepancy. Invite someone to challenge your explanation. Build with people who will make your work stronger, and publish enough for the next person to continue. A shared capacity to question the numbers begins with contributions like yours.
